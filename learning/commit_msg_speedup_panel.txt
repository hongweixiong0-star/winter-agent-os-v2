knowledge(ui): the model was right about the speedup panel, and the record was missing

_The refusal said the answer was wrong. It was not: it said the answer was not on record._

The live reply, `learning/local_planner_steps.jsonl`, 2026-10-08T03:31:49Z, on the 士兵训练加速 panel:

    {"decision": "EXECUTE", "action": {"type": "CLICK_ELEMENT", "target_element_id": "E16"},
     "expected": {"result": "training_completed"},
     "reason": "Goal is to keep building productive; '一键使用' (Use All) is the only executable
                control to apply boosts and finish training"}

-- and the contract answered `PLAN_TARGET_IS_NOT_A_CONTROL: E16 is TEXT_LABEL`.  The model named the
control in English *and* Chinese and was refused for not having a word for it.

**Getting back to the frame.**  `ui_venus_online` / `local_planner_steps` record
`frame_id = frame_<sha256[:16]>` of the image bytes and no path, so the picture behind a call could
only be found by hashing candidates in the episode's own directory.  Getting to the right directory
was half the work: the frames live under `dataset/raw/control_panel/runtime_auto/<episode_id>/`, and
a first look under `dataset/raw/live_runtime/` returned nothing, which is how a wrong "the frame was
pruned" conclusion gets made.  Measured afterwards: of 20,177 distinct frames the episode ledger
cites, **20,177 are on disk**.  The frame is
`.../20261008_112917_779019_step_007_before_20261008T033042567078.png`, 287,850 bytes, matching the
call's own recorded `image_bytes` exactly.

**The reproduction, on that frame, with the registry as it then stood:** 16 elements, every one a
`TEXT_LABEL`, `executable_count 0` -- the ledger's own `element_count 16 / executable_count 0` -- and
E16 really is `一键使用`.  So the typing failure is reproduced from the registry rather than argued
from it, and the word the model wanted is named.

**What is registered:** `一键使用` and its sibling `使用`, both `label_is_the_control`, so nothing
stored is a coordinate -- the region is the box this frame's OCR measured for the word.  The sample
is one frame each and the entries say so (`occurrences_reviewed`), because the honesty of "is it ever
plain text elsewhere" rests on one observation here, not on a corpus sweep.

**What is refused on purpose:** `立即完成`, on the same panel.  It prints a gem cost -- 8,292 there,
15,699 on 城墙 -- and it is the orange gem-cost pill in both.  `OPERATIONAL.md` puts 大额宝石 in the
Strict tier (门禁不放宽, §8) and `PRINCIPLES.md` requires 钻石 to pass the Risk Gate when
unauthorised, so registering it would be a gate removal by registry entry.  The refusal is written
*into the file* (`refused_on_purpose`) and asserted by a test, so the next pass cannot re-add it as a
"sibling of the two that were just added".

**The end-to-end measurement, with the project's own instrument**
(`tools/benchmark_gui_unknown.py`, seven cases):

    before  INVALID_ACTION_RATE 0.5714   ELEMENT_ID_VALIDITY null   element_plans 0
    after   INVALID_ACTION_RATE 0.4286   ELEMENT_ID_VALIDITY 1.0    element_plans 1

and the case itself, same words in the same model's reason:

    [7/7] UNKNOWN::使用  elements=16  decision=EXECUTE  basis=FRAME_ELEMENT  target=E16  parse_error=""

`ELEMENT_ID_VALIDITY 1.0` is the element path's whole guarantee and it was `null` before because no
element-naming plan had ever survived.  `SAFE_DEFER_RATE` moves 1.0 -> 0.75, which is the intended
direction and is only correct because this screen genuinely has an actionable control that the run's
own goal wants.

**One thing the acceptance does not say, stated rather than left to be discovered.**  Ids are
positional (`E1..En` in emission order, composites first), so adding two controls renumbered this
frame's table: the accepted plan's `target_element_id` is E16, which in the *new* table is the label
copy of `使用`, while the model's reason still names `一键使用`.  Both are real buttons on the panel
and both are now typed as controls, so the tap lands on the screen the model meant; the exact word is
not the one its reason says.  The reuse path is unaffected because it is anchored on text, not on an
id -- measured in its own payload: `grounding_basis: CURRENT_FRAME_OCR`, `ocr_anchor: word`.

**The record that made the case reviewable.**  `learning/unknown_requests/unknown__control__bc905ae7.json`
is filed for this screen, which also puts its frame under the UNKNOWN evidence stream's protection.
Filed first *without* `ocr_texts`/`ocr_boxes`, and the benchmark then showed the model a screen with
**0 elements** -- the case was not the case, and it made the first run's aggregate look worse than the
code is.  Re-read with the project's own OCR service: 16 regions, matching the live refusal's own
`element_count 16`.  That mistake is in the commit rather than silently corrected, because "the
record must carry the reading the call was made from" is the kind of thing this project keeps
re-learning.

**Evidence-integrity found on the way, and left alone:**
* the UNKNOWN protection stream held only the 15 manual sweepcases and **nothing filed by the live
  calls**, so a live refusal whose episode rotates out of the 10,000-row ledger becomes unreviewable
  -- measured: 5,447 of 6,615 episode directories are now empty, and 8 of the 15 sweepcase records no
  longer have their frame;
* the four remaining refusals on the `QUICK_PANEL_ROW_BUILDING` screen (2026-10-01/02) are already in
  that state, so they are **recorded as unreviewable rather than guessed at**.

**Proof.**  `tests/test_ui_collection.py::TheSpeedupPanelTests`, four cases, and the bite is shown
rather than claimed: removing the two registry entries fails three of the four and leaves the gem
refusal green in **both** directions -- the negative control does not depend on the additions.  A/B
over the eight suites that read this registry: **23 failed with the entries, 27 without**, and the
four that differ are exactly these tests; the other 23 are byte-identical in both states.  The file's
own formatting was preserved (indent=1), so the diff is 51 lines rather than a 887-line reflow of a
file nobody could then review.
